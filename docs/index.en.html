落

I Ching, cast by real physics

Shake your phone and three Kaiyuan Tongbao coins tumble across the desk and settle. Six throws build a hexagram, shown beside its transformation, with the classical Zhou Yi text and a plain-language reading.

Luò runs entirely offline. No account, no network, no data collected. For entertainment only.

How to cast

Tap 六爻 on the home screen, then shake your phone (or tap the cast button at the bottom). When the three coins settle they are read by the traditional three-coin method — blank face is yang, inscribed face is yin — one throw per line, six throws to a hexagram. The faces are spelled out on screen after each throw, so you never have to squint at the coins.

掷币 is a single-coin yes/no cast, good for warming up.

Questions

Shaking does nothing.
Luò judges the throw by what the coins actually do: all three have to genuinely tumble for the throw to count as a line. A throw where they barely move is a free one — it isn't recorded. That way every line comes from a real toss rather than from how hard you shook. Try a flick from the wrist.

Where is my cast log stored? Does it sync?
On your device only. Nothing is uploaded and nothing syncs. You can delete casts individually or clear them all from the Cast Log screen. See the privacy policy.

What is the dimmed line under the classical text?
A plain-language gloss. The classical text is reproduced faithfully and never altered; the gloss sits on its own line so the two never blur together.
